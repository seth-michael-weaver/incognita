# INCOGNITA R-matrix evaluation of n + C-12 (elastic, 10 µeV to 1.80 MeV)

Seth Michael Weaver and the INCOGNITA project, 2026-11-07. This is the public summary of the evaluation. The curve shown
on the Every nuclide page (data.html#C-12/el) is `C12RM9.json` in this folder; `cmp/C12RM9.json` is the comparison
layer of the same page.

## Limits and caveats
- The fit is not a clean fit: in-sample χ²/dof is 3.10 (total cross sections 3.87 per point). Two data sets need strong
  tilts and are flagged (EXFOR 11326002, tilt pull 6.9; 10050002, 3.1); they were kept, not dropped.
- The acceptance checks below are calibrated (a perfect model passes all of them together with probability ≥ 0.95), but
  their power is low: the IAEA-standard checks would pass a model off by a uniform 0.5 % about 97 % of the time. Passing
  shows the evaluation is consistent with the data and the standard; it is not a strong test.
- Earlier attempts at C-12 did not pass these checks; this evaluation is the first that does, and the IAEA standard
  was consulted more than once along the way.
- ENDF/B-VIII.1 scores about the same on the total cross-section data not used in the fit (χ²/N 0.959 vs ours 0.958;
  report only).
- Only elastic scattering is evaluated. Capture is not modelled. Nothing above 1.80 MeV is ours.
- The criteria of the NJOY round-trip check were revised twice, because the first two versions were miscalibrated
  (unmodified ENDF/B-VIII.1 failed them too, or could not test them). The final version passes with a proper control;
  its criteria were chosen after seeing the earlier numbers.

## What it is
A multi-level R-matrix (Brune parametrisation, channel radius 4.0 fm) of n + C-12, fitted to 127 EXFOR data sets
(8508 points after rebinning: total and elastic cross sections and elastic angular distributions of natural carbon and
C-12, 10 eV to 1.95 MeV) plus an anchor from the measured coherent scattering length (b = 6.6535(14) fm, giving 4.7450(20) b). Each set has its own
normalisation and tilt; the absolute total-cross-section sets share one scale c_TOT = 0.9938(17) and slope. Natural
carbon is modelled as 98.93 % C-12 plus a fixed C-13 component (comparator use only, not part of the curve).
The published curve is 0 K, pure C-12, elastic, 10 µeV to 1.80 MeV (the range the IAEA carbon standard covers), with
the mean centre-of-mass scattering cosine from the same model.

## Processing and integral checks
- **ENDF-6 assembly: PASS.** A test-vehicle ENDF-6 file (our elastic and angular distribution below 1.80 MeV, a linear
  blend to ENDF/B-VIII.1 over 1.80-1.95 MeV, ENDF/B-VIII.1 everywhere else including capture) reproduces the fit to
  1.9e-5. It is a test vehicle for processing and benchmarks only, not a released file, because it carries
  evaluated-library content.
- **NJOY round trip: PASS.** 0 K reconstruction 1.9e-5 from the fit, 294 K 1.05e-3 above 1 eV, mean scattering cosine
  within 1.8e-4; a re-gridded ENDF/B-VIII.1 control passes the same checks.
- **Integral benchmark PCI-001 (plutonium-graphite system, ICSBEP pu-comp-inter-001): PASS.** With only C-12 replaced by
  the test vehicle, k = 0.99799(24) vs 0.99777(24) for ENDF/B-VIII.1 on the same machine: Δk = +22 ± 34 pcm, and
  |C − E| = 201 pcm ≤ 223 + 2 × 34 pcm. The benchmark's own uncertainty is 1100 pcm, so this shows the new elastic does
  not disturb a graphite-bearing system; it is not evidence that it improves one.
