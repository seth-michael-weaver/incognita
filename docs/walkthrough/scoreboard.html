    2 · The data product (TERRA) against evaluated libraries

    TERRA takes the engine's curves and, where good measurements exist, anchors the result to them, with an uncertainty band. It is compared with the four major evaluated libraries on accuracy and on whether its uncertainty bands cover the data as often as they claim.

    How to read it

    Gap
    For each nucleus in a comparison, the typical error against measurement is taken in log10 units (dex; 0.01 dex ≈ 2.3 %). The gap is our error minus the rival's on the same nuclei. Negative = we are closer to the data.
    WIN, TIE, LOSS
    The 95 % confidence interval of the gap comes from resampling the nuclei. WIN: the whole interval is below zero. LOSS: the whole interval is above zero. TIE: the interval contains zero, so the data cannot tell the two apart, whatever the point estimate says.
    Trail, parity, underpowered
    Kinds of tie. Trail: we are behind on the point estimate. Parity: the two are close. Underpowered: we lead on the point estimate, but there are too few nuclei to be sure.
    Class A
    The engine is compared on equal terms only with other model calculations (class A). Evaluated libraries are fitted to the same measurements, so they are not counted against the engine; TERRA, which also uses the measurements, is the product judged against them.
    The rivals
    stock: TALYS-2 with its standard input. R_T ("recipe R"): the same physics run the way the TENDL library is made, without per-nucleus fitted inputs. CoH3 and YAHFC: two independent Hauser–Feshbach reaction codes. TERRA's rivals are the evaluated libraries ENDF/B-VIII.1, JEFF-4.0, JENDL-5 and TENDL-2025.
    MACS bands
    Maxwellian-averaged capture cross sections, the stellar capture rates, grouped by proton number Z. They are scored on two label sets (126 and 185 nuclides) that give identical verdicts, so each band counts once.
